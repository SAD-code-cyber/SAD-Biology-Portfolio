01. Osmotic Trigger

Sweat makes the extracellular space hypertonic

  • 1. Sweat Loss

    Sweat removes water from the extracellular compartment.

  • 2. ECF Osmolarity Rises

    Solutes become more concentrated: ECF osmolarity rises.

  • 3. Water Efflux

    Hypertonic ECF pulls water out of cells down its water-potential gradient.

Key Mechanism: Sweat loss → Extracellular hypertonicity → Aquaporin-mediated water efflux → Cell volume falls.
02. Cell-Type Response

Same osmotic gradient, different cell mechanics

Plant Cell

Cellulose Wall
(Central Vacuole Empties)

TURGOR ↓
Shape retained by rigid cellulose wall; membrane loses outward pressure ↓.

Human Cell

Crenation
(No Cell Wall)

MEMBRANE DEFORMATION
Volume and surface contour shrink freely as water exits.

Shared Cause: Hypertonic ECF draws water outward; structural constraints determine the visible response. Plant turgor pressure falls against a rigid wall, whereas the wall-free human membrane crenates.
